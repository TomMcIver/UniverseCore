# Game Vision

## Core fantasy

The player inhabits a procedurally generated, physically grounded universe beginning around Bronze/Iron Age technological conditions. The player can participate in, accelerate, obstruct, exploit, or ignore civilization. The world does not wait for the player.

Civilizations are populated by autonomous simulated people who are born, age, form relationships, learn, migrate, create institutions, discover technologies, fight, cooperate, reproduce, and die. The player does not age normally but can die. Early continuity is rebirth into the ongoing world; later continuity can become technologically mediated through cloning, backups, synthetic bodies, distributed consciousness, or other systems justified by the world's technology.

The long-term scope extends from local villages to cities, nations, planets, star systems, galaxies, universe-scale engineering, and potentially traversal between procedurally generated universes.

## Design pillars

### Persistent history

Consequences persist. Civilizations continue while the player is absent. Knowledge can spread, mutate, be preserved, or be lost. Institutions may outlive their founders. The player can return centuries later to consequences they did not directly observe.

### Real scale where meaningful

Planets and astronomical distances should represent meaningful real-scale relationships. The implementation may use nested coordinate spaces, procedural generation, streaming, and abstraction rather than a single enormous coordinate system.

### Interaction-driven resolution

Reality is resolved according to causal need.

A beach can exist as an aggregate granular field until the player picks up sand. At that point relevant grains become individual physical entities. Once settled and irrelevant, those grains can collapse back into aggregate state while preserving the resulting surface changes.

The same principle applies to:

- populations
- ecosystems
- cities
- weather
- water
- debris
- planetary systems
- cognition

### Emergent civilization

There is no mandatory Earth-history timeline. A society's technological path depends on available materials, environment, institutions, accumulated knowledge, individual discoveries, communication and physical feasibility.

A player who knows modern science can attempt to bootstrap advanced technology early, but knowledge alone is insufficient: precision tooling, purification, measurement, energy, supply chains and other prerequisites must exist.

### Alien worlds generated from constraints

Alien planets should not be recoloured Earths. Gravity, atmosphere, chemistry, geology, climate and evolutionary constraints should influence organisms, ecosystems, architectures, technologies and civilizations.

### Multiverse without arbitrary magic

Speculative technology is allowed when based on plausible or theoretically motivated extrapolation. The game may choose concrete fictional implementations for uncertain physics, but internal rules must remain consistent and discoverable.

## Player continuity

The player is persistent across history but not physically invulnerable.

Early game death may cause rebirth into a different household in the same continuing civilization. Rebirth does not preserve political office. Economic continuity should not be an unexplained magical bank account; over time it should increasingly arise from persistent institutions, inherited structures, trusts, organizations, stored instructions, technology, or other world mechanisms.

## Time

Civilization requires accelerated passage of time, but acceleration must not become a tactical cheat. The model should distinguish:

- real-time local simulation
- coarse-grained simulation while the player is inactive
- explicit waiting/stasis
- technologically enabled suspended animation or subjective-time manipulation

The player cannot both act at normal granularity and arbitrarily accelerate everyone else.

## Population

Population must be regional and demographic rather than controlled by a single global birth rate.

Local fertility, mortality and migration should emerge from factors such as:

- age structure
- nutrition
- disease
- wealth
- culture
- family structure
- housing
- education
- contraception
- infant mortality
- war
- environmental conditions
- policy
- transport
- economic opportunity

This allows one region's population to shrink while global population grows.

## NPC cognition

NPCs exist at multiple cognitive resolutions. Most people should not run continuous language models. Durable NPC state includes identity, health, relationships, possessions, memories, knowledge, beliefs, skills, goals and social position.

High-cost reasoning is invoked only when needed, for example conversation, invention, diplomacy, strategy or other consequential decisions.

## End-state ambition

The final ambition is not merely "many planets". It is a universe capable of producing histories, civilizations and physical situations that neither the player nor developer explicitly scripted, while remaining sufficiently coherent that the player can investigate how they arose.
